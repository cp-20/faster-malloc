# Round 6 — page-granular return: the bursty gap closes, and three honest verdicts

*EN summary: Round 6 took round 5's backlog in order. (1) The
census now returns individual 4 KiB pages inside partially-free
spans (ADR 0030): bursty idle-2 RSS falls from 88.2 MB to
**44.2 MB — best in the field**, ahead of mimalloc (54.8) and
glibc (106.3). The design that shipped is the second one: the
first (run containment) captured 0.4 MB of a 44 MB ceiling, and
the measurements forced the correct invariant — a page is
purgeable when every block intersecting it is free in central
and every block starting in it is free in central (the start
count is what makes it safe against other threads' local lists).
(2) Lua's peak is decomposed (ADR 0031): only 40% is live data;
the rest is the large-cache growth trail (~3.1 MB), baseline
(~1.4 MB) and cross-class span residues (~6.7 MB) — returnable,
not leaked, and the footprint profile provably cannot move this
workload. (3) Zero-trickle retention is measured (ADR 0032):
every allocator in the field sits flat while fully silent, and
the opt-in `FM_IDLE_RELEASE=1` timer thread makes v2 the only
one that returns memory in silence (41.1 → 29.5 MB). (4) In-place
realloc extension was built, strace-verified and **rejected**
(ADR 0033): 1,955 attempts across rgrow and sqlite, zero
successes — the allocator's own packed mappings always occupy
the end address.*

## The short version

Round 5 ended with one clear loss: after a burst drains, the
bursty daemon sat at 88.2 MB idle where mimalloc sat at 53.6 —
because v2 returned memory only in whole 64 KiB spans and
mimalloc returns pages. Round 6 closes that loss and turns it
into a win, then spends the rest of its budget on the remaining
backlog with the same rule as always: measure first, adopt only
what the numbers carry, and write the ADR either way. Two items
landed (page purge; the idle-release prototype behind a flag),
one item closed as a decomposition with no code change (lua),
and one was built and rejected on evidence (realloc extension).

A note on the session itself: the VM was replaced mid-round,
wiping /tmp (an in-flight app matrix and the A/B library) and
the system packages the app harness uses (redis, lua, jemalloc,
mimalloc, tcmalloc, GNU time). Everything load-bearing was
rebuilt or reinstalled and re-measured on the final build; the
bursty and probe numbers below are from that build. The host
remained a shared, noisy 1.5-CPU VM throughout — within-session
ratios and load-insensitive (RSS) metrics are the readings, as
in every round.

## 1. Page-granular purge (ADR 0030) — the headline

The census learns to `madvise(MADV_DONTNEED)` individual pages
inside spans that still have live blocks. A page qualifies when
two exact counts over the blocks the census holds in central
agree: every block *intersecting* the page is free there, and
every block *starting* in the page is free there. The first
protects live data; the second protects the free lists — a
block's link word is its first bytes, so a block starting in a
purged page must be one the census itself seized and withholds
from circulation. Withheld blocks are exactly "blocks starting
in purged pages", a pure function of the span's mask, so reclaim
re-carves them arithmetically when a span's circulating supply
runs dry. Decisions age like dead-span marks; `malloc_trim`
purges instantly. Knobs: `FM_PAGE_PURGE`, `FM_PURGE_MIN_PAGES`.

Three things the measurements caught on the way:

- **The first design was wrong.** Requiring purged pages to form
  runs of wholly-contained blocks captured ~0.4 MB of a 44 MB
  ceiling: with a 64-byte header offset, nearly every page
  boundary has a straddling block, and the edge-shrink rule ate
  every run. Containment confused "a free block's tail bytes are
  discarded" (harmless) with "its link word is discarded"
  (fatal). The start-count rule is the correct invariant and is
  simpler — no runs at all.
- **Purge findings starved the census cadence twice**, in two
  different ways: first by holding the release interval at its
  floor (bursty's drain went from never finishing to 0.2 s of
  census CPU once purge waits got their own counter), then by
  charging coverage walks to the walked counter that prices the
  cadence (consprobe fell from 9 censuses to 2 and settled at
  17.7 MB; restored to 8.9 MB once coverage stopped being
  charged — it is bounded by the candidate mass it can return).
- **The ceiling is real.** A diagnostic fork of bursty
  (bursty2) that computes the exact purgeable bytes from live
  addresses at idle-2 shows ~44 MB of fully-free pages inside
  partial spans — the mechanism now captures essentially all of
  what the geometry offers (trim reaches 43.6 MB RSS vs 86.2 MB
  without purge).

## 2. Lua's peak, decomposed (ADR 0031)

An instrumented fork of the workload (churn2.lua) prints Lua's
own live-byte count next to RSS at each phase boundary. At the
peak, live data is 7.64 MB of v2's 18.9 MB. The 11.2 MB excess
over live: ~3.1 MB of large-cache growth trail (phase 3's
realloc-doubling superseded blocks — it vanishes with
`FM_LCACHE_MAX_BYTES=0`), ~1.4 MB of baseline, ~6.7 MB of
partially-free spans from phase-2 classes that phase 3 cannot
reuse. jemalloc's excess is 9.3 MB; glibc and mimalloc sit at
~4.5. All of it is returnable retention, none of it live or
leaked — but a 0.8 s batch process gives ageing no window, so
the honest score is: lua remains a **time win and a peak-RSS
loss**, with the mechanism named. The footprint-profile
contradiction resolves too: the profile's cache cap (8 MB)
still covers the 3.1 MB trail and its eager release has no dead
span to release mid-run, so it cannot move this workload —
balanced vs footprint snapshots agree within 200 KB at every
phase. ADR 0024's open item is closed.

## 3. Zero trickle, and a timer thread behind a flag (ADR 0032)

New cell idleprobe: ramp ~92 MB, drain to ~12 MB, then six
seconds of total silence. **Every allocator is flat for the
whole silence** (glibc 36.7, v2 40.8, mimalloc 43.5 MB) — all
release machinery in the field is activity-driven, so a silent
process keeps its drain-end level indefinitely. That was ADR
0029's reopening measurement, and its design 1 is now
prototyped: `FM_IDLE_RELEASE=1` (default off) spawns a detached
timer thread on the first flush tick; after 1 s without a tick
it runs the ordinary ageing censuses, and keeps sweeping once a
second while silence lasts. On idleprobe it is the only entry
that moves: **41.1 → 29.5 MB during silence**. An atfork child
handler re-arms it after fork (verified with a fork+churn+idle
test). It stays default-off for ADR 0029's reasons: a thread in
every process is a real embedding cost, and the harm case is
long-lived daemons, which can opt in.

## 4. Realloc mapping extension — rejected (ADR 0033)

The prototype glued an anonymous run at a large mapping's end
with `MAP_FIXED_NOREPLACE` before falling back to alloc+copy:
safe by construction, battery-green. It never fired. Strace
counts: rgrow 1,600 attempts / 0 successes; sqlite speedtest
355 / 0; lua's peak unchanged. The allocator's own heap is
densely packed — small spans are mappings too, the kernel places
large mappings adjacently, and the large cache retains freed
mappings in the same neighbourhood — so a growing block's end
address is essentially always occupied. Reverted (af80cec); the
ADR keeps the evidence and the surviving observation (the large
cache's two-bucket search is why the growth trail never
recycles — a smaller idea, deferred to round 7 unless a workload
promotes it).

The shape is round 5's, unchanged by the purge: v2 is at or near
the top on time everywhere (sqlite's single-rep delta is inside
the noise band ADR 0023 documented for this VM), and the peak-RSS
column keeps its known split — v2 heavier on the short-lived
apps, best-in-field on every freed-then-idle instrument (§1, §3).
No cell regressed in a way attributable to round 6.

Per-use-case verdicts for v2 (balanced), updated for round 6:

- **Dominates:** bursty idle-2 (44.2 MB, best of the field;
  idle-1 session-variable, 52–78 MB across this round's runs),
  consprobe (8.9 MB settled), decayprobe.
  With `FM_IDLE_RELEASE=1`, also the only allocator that
  returns memory in total silence.
- **Wins:** lua on time; macro suite broadly (compiler, jsondoc,
  game best; kvstore/locality at the top with jit).
- **Ties:** the app matrix's time columns vs glibc (within
  session noise, as in round 5).
- **Still loses, with reasons:**
  - *Peak RSS on short-lived apps* (lua +63% vs glibc):
    decomposed in §2 — designed retention (large-cache trail)
    plus class segregation, in a window too short for ageing.
    Unchanged in kind from round 5; now fully accounted.
  - *rgrow-style doubling* (v2 ~0.20 s vs glibc ~0.04 s):
    alloc+copy stands; the in-place alternative is measured
    impossible in this heap layout (§4). A synthetic pattern;
    sqlite's real cell stays noise-bound (ADR 0023).
  - *Peak RSS during the burst itself* (bursty peak 112.2 vs
    glibc 106.1 on this session's rebuilt binary): the peak is
    live working set plus caches; the round's wins are all in
    what happens after the burst.

The shape after six rounds: v2 is never the wrong choice on
time, is now the best choice in every freed-then-idle scenario
measured (trickle or, with the flag, silent), and remains
heavier at the instantaneous peak of brief dense processes —
retention that is accounted byte-for-byte rather than
mysterious.

## 6. Tests

Full battery on the final (post-revert) build: test_basic under
v2 and JIT and under all three FM_PROFILEs, stress ×3, check,
mt in both modes, earlyinit, tlsexit — all green, plus
test_basic with `FM_IDLE_RELEASE=1` and the fork test from
ADR 0032. FM_DEBUG builds pass test_basic, stress and bursty2
with every census assert silent (purge skip counts, reclaim
counts, regroup totals, effective-free overcount). The purge
work also survived a mid-round infinite-loop hunt (a census
finalize livelock, fixed by resuming the run scan at the
original run end — the code that replaced the run logic removed
the whole class of bug; the regression shape is covered by
bursty2 completing under the debug build).

## 7. Round-7 backlog

1. **nginx/pgbench/ruby cells re-run on a quiet box** — round 6's
   sessions were never quiet enough to refresh round 5's ratios
   (item 5 of this round's backlog, explicitly conditional; the
   bursty and probe numbers above are load-insensitive and are
   the round's real evidence).
2. **Large-cache bucket search on the realloc path** (ADR 0033's
   survivor): widen the search when the caller is realloc, where
   the freed predecessor's size is known to bracket the request;
   adopt only if a real workload (not rgrow alone) shows it.
3. **Purge under contention**: the purge was validated mostly on
   single-threaded shapes; an mt-shaped bursty variant would
   exercise the start-count veto under real cross-thread local
   lists.
4. **FM_IDLE_RELEASE hardening** if the prototype earns it:
   exit-time behaviour in processes that dlclose, and a
   spawn-once audit for early-init edge cases.
5. Demo: the round-6 row joins the app-matrix chart data.
