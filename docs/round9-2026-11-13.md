## Reclamation prototype

ADR [0041](adr/0041-synchronous-epoch-reclamation.md) designs and prototypes
synchronous global reader epochs for central batch headers, starting from
`d9c4364` on `round9/reclamation`. CAS push/pop execute in reader sections;
a successful pop waits for a grace period before header reuse. A census closes
admission and drains readers before detaching, then reopens before walking.
The census mutex remains necessary for span purge/withheld metadata. The
compile-time `FM_RECLAIM_EPOCH` experiment is absent from default builds;
normal v2 `.text` matches the untouched locked baseline byte-for-byte.

Five alternating measured pairs per case after one warmup pair, same O3 compiler
and hardware (shared Xeon 8573C VM, two-CPU quota). Ownership is the unchanged
four-pair, 64-class harness, 800,000 transfers per run, with endpoint/count
assertions. All final samples pass. Bursty is the existing app-matrix workload;
all checksums match, and its sleeps dominate wall time. Full samples, phase RSS,
host/compiler details and discarded preliminary data are in
[`bench/results/round9-reclamation/`](../bench/results/round9-reclamation/).

**Reject adoption; no verified overall win.** Local regresses with nonoverlapping
ranges; many-class median regresses substantially. Cross-free/census ranges
overlap widely and the preliminary series reverses their median direction.
The global reader cache line and per-successful-pop synchronous grace period
have not earned replacement of the locks. Retain only the disabled prototype
and regression test. No defaults, purge geometry or runtime profiles change.

Exit needs no reader registry or retirement destructor: each short epoch ends
within the allocator operation, including cache publication on TLS exit.
Deferred cancellation is outside the reader sections; asynchronous cancellation
inside malloc remains unsupported. A stalled reader pins the global grace
period; admission closes and reclaimers/fork wait without a timeout or unsafe
reclamation. Fork prepare takes census then central ownership and drains
readers; both parent/child reopen with zero readers, and the child's timer
stays disabled until exec. Vanished threads' privately owned batches can remain
unreachable in a child, as with baseline TLS caches; they are never guessed free.
The ADR covers memory ordering, ABA, lock order and fork/registration limitations.

The first exploratory helper-control series suffered a bursty SIGSEGV and
changed default code layout. It is retained and excluded from the exact-control
final medians; original/prototype standalone reruns and all final bursty runs
passed. No root cause is claimed for that preliminary failure.

Validation: `make -B -j4` and the complete `bash bench/round7.sh` pass
for both locked and epoch builds, including v2/JIT, all profiles, stress,
realloccache, idlefork, early initialization, TLS exit and the idlelifecycle
fault matrix. The epoch define was propagated into custom lifecycle DSOs.
The new deterministic stalled-reader grace test passes and is included in
round7.sh. All twelve FM_DEBUG stress runs pass (three per implementation and
configuration), with `FM_RELEASE_AGE_MS=0`, `FM_PURGE_MIN_PAGES=1`,
`FM_IDLE_RELEASE=1` and `FM_REALLOC_CACHE_SEARCH=1`. Default DSOs were rebuilt
afterward. Shell/Python syntax, all 60 final sample counts/checksums and
`git diff --check` pass.

The first locked battery stopped with SIGBUS in JIT's footprint-profile
`test_basic`; this DSO's machine code matched untouched main. Five subsequent
repetitions of all three profiles against the original JIT DSO passed, followed
by the complete locked and epoch batteries. The initial failure and diagnostic
outputs are retained under `bench/results/round9-reclamation/failures/`.
It remains unexplained; passing reruns do not establish its cause or resolution.
No exhaustive race-detector or quiet reference-host validation is claimed.
